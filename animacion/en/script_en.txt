This cockroach can feel you move before you even blink. And tonight, two brothers are trapped in its kitchen.

The capsules spin, and spin, and spin, and stop. Cockroach.

Good luck, brothers, you are going to need it.

Twenty days ago, Javier and Leo escaped the garden house, running from a mosquito the size of a grown man. Since then, they have not stopped moving. The radios are dead, and almost nobody answers.

The world is falling apart. The cities are silent. The streets are empty. The cars are abandoned with their doors wide open. Humanity is almost gone. And the few who are left hide so well that you never hear them.

Day twenty.

The brothers have not eaten in two days. The last can is gone. Leo has stopped making jokes. Javier has stopped giving orders. They just walk, at night, dodging everything that buzzes, everything that crawls, everything that moves in the shadows.

And then, at the end of an empty street, they see a restaurant. Broken glass. A fallen sign. And at the back, behind the counter, a metal door left half open. The kitchen.

A kitchen means food. Cans. Water. Maybe their very last chance.

Javier thinks about it. Leo does not. Leo is already inside, opening cans in his head.

The kitchen smells of damp and old grease. Light barely gets in through one tiny window, covered in dirt. There are pots hanging from the ceiling, metal tables, and a row of black stoves, covered in dust.

Everything is quiet. Too quiet.

Leo moves forward with the flashlight. Javier follows, pressed against the wall. At the back there is a heavy door. The pantry. And behind it, shelves packed with cans, bottles of water and sealed sacks.

Leo whispers: Javier, we are saved.

Javier does not answer. He is looking at the floor. There are marks. Small scratches, like dry claw marks, running from one side of the kitchen to the other. And a sweet, strange smell that he cannot place.

They open a can of tomatoes and eat with their hands, sitting on the pantry floor. It is the best thing they have tasted in weeks.

And then they hear it.

Tick, tick, tick.

A dry sound. Like tiny nails on metal. It comes from somewhere in the kitchen. It gets closer. It stops. And it starts again.

Javier turns off the flashlight. The two of them freeze.

Javier remembers something. Cockroaches love dark, warm and humid places. And they eat almost anything. Leftovers, crumbs, grease, even paper. For a cockroach, an abandoned kitchen is not a sad place. It is paradise.

If a cockroach lives here, this is its home.

Leo dares to peek out of the pantry door. He looks at the stoves. And in the narrow gap between two of them, something moves.

First, a thin tip. Then another. Two huge antennae, longer than an arm, slowly sliding out of the crack and feeling the air. They sweep to one side. Then the other. As if they were reading the room.

And then Javier realizes the impossible. That crack is far too thin for a creature that size. But cockroaches can flatten their bodies to squeeze through gaps you would never believe.

That means that, for her, there are no walls. There is no hiding place. There is no safe corner.

The antennae keep feeling the air. Closer and closer to the pantry.

Day twenty one.

The brothers did not sleep. They took turns keeping watch, backs against the door, while the tick, tick, tick crawled around the kitchen all night long.

At first light, Leo has an idea. They have seen the antennae. Now they need to see the whole thing.

Javier says no. But hunger, exhaustion and fear do their work. Leo switches on the flashlight and sweeps the kitchen with the beam.

And then it happens.

Something huge, shiny and reddish brown shoots out from under a table. It is so fast it looks like it teleports. It crosses the floor, climbs the wall and vanishes behind the fridge, all in less than a second.

Leo stands there with his mouth open. Javier, with his heart in his throat.

But Javier noticed something important. It did not attack. It ran. The moment the light touched it, it hid.

Most cockroaches are nocturnal. They avoid light as if it hurt them. And that gives the brothers an idea. If light scares it away, light can be their shield.

Leo gets brave. He walks through the kitchen sweeping every corner with the flashlight. Every time the beam touches a shadow, something moves and hides. It is like playing hide and seek with a monster.

Until Leo makes a mistake. He runs after a shadow to get a closer look. He gets near the fridge. He turns. He slips on the greasy floor.

The flashlight goes flying. It bounces off a metal table. And it breaks in two.

The light flickers once. And it dies.

The kitchen goes dark.

From some corner, very close, the tick, tick, tick starts again.

And this time, it does not sound afraid.

Day twenty two.

Without light, the kitchen is a different place. The brothers can only see the gray strip of the window. Everything else is black.

Javier tries to think. There is something he does not understand. Every time they ran, the creature reacted before they ever reached it. It could not see them. It could not hear them. But it knew exactly where they were.

Then he remembers. Cockroaches have, at the back of their bodies, two tiny sensors that feel movements in the air. Any draft, no matter how small. And they react in a few thousandths of a second, many times faster than you can blink.

That is why they are so hard to catch. It does not need to see you. It only needs you to move.

Javier looks at Leo. And he says something Leo never thought he would hear: we have to move as if we were underwater.

They spend the whole morning practicing. One step, wait. Another step, wait. Arms tight against the body. Slow breathing. No sudden moves. It is exhausting. But it works. For the first time, crossing the kitchen, there is not a single tick. Not one shadow moves.

In the afternoon, Leo decides to go to the pantry for water. He moves slowly, with the bottles in his arms. He almost makes it.

But just as he turns back, a gust of wind blows in through the broken window. The pantry door, barely hanging on, slams shut. A dry crack that booms like thunder.

Leo is stuck inside. Javier, outside.

Javier tries to open it. He cannot. A latch has fallen on the other side and the door is far too heavy. He knocks softly, trying not to make noise. And then he hears something behind him.

Tick, tick, tick.

Very close.

He turns around. And in the half light, just a few steps away, he sees two long antennae slowly moving toward the pantry door.

Inside, Leo holds his breath. Outside, Javier does not dare move a finger.

And the creature just stays there, touching the door, as if it knows someone is on the other side.

Day twenty three.

Leo is safe, but trapped. He has cans and water, so he will not starve. Even so, hour after hour, the creature stays posted right by the door.

Javier cannot stop thinking. He needs to draw it away. And there is a way. Cockroaches can survive weeks without food, but only a few days without water. That creature needs to drink. And in the kitchen there is a broken pipe dripping next to the fridge. That is its source.

If Javier sets up another source of water far away, with some bread crumbs, the creature will go there. And the door will be free.

He prepares the plan very carefully. He moves a metal cart to make room. He pushes it toward the back wall. He puts a bowl of water and pieces of bread on a table, on the other side of the kitchen. And he hides, without a sound, behind some sacks.

He waits.

One minute. Ten. An hour.

Finally, the antennae appear. The creature leaves the pantry and heads for the bowl. It stops. It drinks. It eats.

The plan works.

Javier slips out of his hiding place, slowly, step by step, as if underwater. He reaches the pantry. He lifts the latch. Leo is about to come out.

But then Javier sees something he had missed. The metal cart he moved is now pushed right up against the wall. Right in front of a dark hole, behind the fridge.

That is the creature's exit. Its way in and its way out. And Javier just blocked it.

The cockroach finishes drinking and turns toward its path. It stops. It feels the cart with its antennae. It pushes. It does not move.

And for the first time, it does not run. It thrashes. It slams the cart with its whole body. The pots crash down from the ceiling. The shelves shake. The whole kitchen rumbles.

Javier grabs Leo and they both dive to the floor, under a table. The noise lasts forever. And when it is over, the cart is knocked over, the kitchen is wrecked, and the creature is still there, perfectly still, in the middle of the mess.

With its antennae raised high. Trembling.

Day twenty four.

The brothers spend the night awake, under the table. And as the sun comes up, Javier finally understands.

He looks back. He goes over everything. The creature hid when they came in. It ran from the light. It moved away every time they moved. It never chased them into the open. It never came for them.

It only fought back once. When they blocked its exit.

The cockroach was never the hunter. It was the owner of the house. And they were the intruders, eating its food, walking on its floor and blocking its path.

The idea is so simple that Javier almost feels embarrassed.

He uses the silence. He moves slowly, as if underwater. He reaches the cart. He pushes it without a sound, inch by inch, until the hole in the wall is free again.

The creature notices. Its antennae twitch. And slowly, very slowly, the cockroach heads for its path. It flattens its body. It slides into the hole. And it disappears.

The kitchen is quiet again.

The brothers take everything they can carry. Cans, water, a flashlight they find in the junk, and the two lightest sacks. Then they look up and see, above the stoves, a big ventilation duct with its grate hanging off.

It is not the front door, but it is a way out. They climb up one after the other and crawl through the duct, in the dark, until they see a light at the end.

They come out on the roof.

It is morning. The sky is clear. From up there you can see the whole city. The buildings, the streets, the stopped cars. Not one voice. Not one radio. Not a single light on.

Nobody is coming to rescue them. Nobody is waiting for them. Nobody knows where they are.

But they are alive. And they have learned something they did not know. Sometimes the monster is not chasing you. Sometimes it is only defending what is its own.

Javier looks at it all in silence. Leo takes a deep breath.

Because real cockroaches, even small ones, carry bacteria on their legs and walk through the dirtiest places in a house. That is why one careless moment in the kitchen can be a real risk. And here, in this story, the danger is even bigger.

The brothers climb down from the roof using a fire escape. They keep walking. Wherever it takes them. Whatever comes next.
